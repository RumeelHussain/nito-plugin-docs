---
title: "What Each Command Protects"
description: "Which Nito commands keep a prompt away from your host's own provider, which do not, and exactly what each one covers."
---

Privacy in the plugin is not one setting; different commands protect different things. This page is the command-by-command map of what leaves your host and what does not.

The key distinction is the [trust boundary](/privacy/overview): your host can see some Nito prompts and not others, independent of the model's privacy tier.

## At a Glance

| Command | Kept from your host's own provider? | Model-tier privacy |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| [`ask`](/commands/ask-and-continue) | No, it runs in your host conversation | Follows the model you choose |
| [`private`](/commands/private) | Yes, that is its purpose | Follows the model you choose |
| [`session`](/commands/session) | Yes, the whole session is Nito-routed | Follows the model you choose |
| [`fusion`](/commands/fusion) | Runs on Nito; see tier-mixing note below | Least-private participant sets the tier |
| [`context`](/commands/context-and-mode) | Controls how much of your prompt's surroundings are sent | Not a model call |

## Ask

`ask` runs **inside your current host conversation**. That means your host can see the `ask` prompt, the same way it sees anything else you type into that session. Its usefulness is model access: it reaches a Nito model without switching your host's model. Use it when the sensitivity of the prompt does not require keeping it from your host.

The Nito model's own privacy tier still applies to the call once it reaches Nito, following the model you chose.

## Private

`private` is the command built for the trust boundary. A `private` turn is **not sent to your host's own model provider**. Be precise about what that covers:

- **What it protects:** the turn is kept out of your host's upstream model provider.
- **What it does not do by itself:** it is not a local sandbox and does not, on its own, stop local disk writes. A no-prompt `private` session is the incognito form that avoids persisting host transcript and prompt history; the options and their honest limits are on the [Private](/commands/private) page.

Pair `private` with a model whose tier matches how sensitive the content is: the command controls what your host sees, and the model controls the tier of the call at Nito.

## Session

A [`session`](/commands/session) is a whole host session powered entirely by a Nito model. Every model turn is Nito-routed rather than going to your host's own provider, for as long as the session runs. Its purpose is sustained work on a Nito model, not the throwaway secrecy of a `private` turn; the two differ on persistence, which the Session page compares directly.

## Fusion

[`fusion`](/commands/fusion) sends one prompt to two or three Nito models. Its privacy has one rule worth calling out: when the participants span different tiers, **the run lands at the least-private tier among them**. A single Anonymous participant pulls the whole Fusion to Anonymous. Keep every participant at the tier you need if the prompt is sensitive.

## Context

[`context`](/commands/context-and-mode) is not a model call and does not change what your host sees. It controls how much of your prompt's surroundings travel with an `ask`: by default only your prompt is sent, and turning context on lets a short generated summary of the task go along. It is prompt-only by default for a reason, so treat turning it on as a deliberate choice.

## The Rule of Thumb

Two questions, two controls:

1. **Should my host see this prompt?** If not, use `private` (or a `session`), not `ask`.
2. **How protected must the call itself be?** Choose a model at the [tier](/privacy/tiers) that matches, since [the tier follows the model](/privacy/tiers).
