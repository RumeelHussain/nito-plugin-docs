description: "The four privacy levels as a ladder, and how the level follows the model you pick."
---

Nito lets you choose how private each call is, and that choice rides on the model you pick rather than being set once for your session. This section is the reference: one page per level, covering what each protects, how you reach it, and what you give up to get it.

## Each Level Builds on the Last

Read the four as a ladder. Each rung adds a stronger guarantee and, in exchange, narrows the models and features available to you. Climb only as high as the call needs.

| Level | How you reach it | What it adds |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Anonymous** | Pick a model whose level is Anonymous | Metadata-only retention by default |
| **Private** | Pick a model whose level is Private | Upstream provider held to zero data retention |
| **Confidential** | Pick a model that offers Confidential | Sealed execution with a verifiable attestation |
| **Encrypted** | Not available in the plugin yet | Client-side encryption to the sealed environment |

## The Level Follows the Model

There is no privacy flag on your command. The level is part of the model's name.

Anonymous and Private are a model's own level, and its ID is just provider and model. A model that also offers Confidential has a second entry in the catalog, whose name ends in `:confidential`.

```text
z-ai/glm-5.2                 the Private entry
z-ai/glm-5.2:confidential    the Confidential entry
```

Two entries, one underlying model, two levels. You pick the level by picking the entry, and you never add the marker yourself: it is part of a name the catalog gives you. See [Model ID Convention](/models/model-id-convention).

Browse with the [`models`](/commands/choosing-a-model) command, then set your model with `model` or `--model`. The level comes with it, which means changing the privacy of a call means changing the model. Nothing else.

## Why a Model Reaches Only Certain Levels

No model offers every level, and the reason is what can be enforced on it.

- **Open-weight models can be served with zero data retention enforced, or run inside confidential hardware.** That is what makes everything above Anonymous possible: retention switched off upstream, sealed execution, an attestation you can check. They start at Private and climb from there.
- **Frontier models always route through their provider.** The most that can be done is make sure the provider never learns who is asking. That is Anonymous, and it is as far as a frontier model goes.

So the ladder is not a quality ranking. It reflects what can be enforced on a given model.

## It Never Silently Downgrades

If a call cannot be served at the chosen model's level, it **fails with a clear message** rather than dropping to a weaker one. A guarantee that quietly weakens is worse than no guarantee, so the call is refused instead.
