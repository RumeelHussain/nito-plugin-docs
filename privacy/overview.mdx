---
title: "How Privacy Works"
description: "Privacy in the Nito plugin comes from two things: which prompts leave your host, and the privacy tier of the model that answers. Both are in your control, and neither silently weakens."
---

Most AI tools treat privacy as one account-wide setting. The Nito plugin does not. Privacy here comes from two independent choices you make, per prompt:

1. **What leaves your host.** Your Claude Code or Codex session normally sends prompts to its own model provider. Nito commands route to Nito instead, and the [`private`](/commands/private) command keeps a turn out of your host's upstream entirely.
2. **The tier of the model that answers.** Every Nito model carries a privacy tier. The tier you get is the tier of the model you choose, and it never silently downgrades.

If you read one sentence, read this: **you choose privacy per prompt, and a stronger tier never quietly weakens into a lesser one.**

## The Trust Boundary

The most important thing to understand is who can see a given prompt.

- Your **host** (Claude Code or Codex) is the program on your machine. In normal use it sends your prompts to its own model provider.
- **Nito** is the service the plugin talks to for its commands.

When you run a Nito command, that prompt goes to Nito. But whether your *host* also sees it depends on the command:

- [`ask`](/commands/ask-and-continue) runs inside your host conversation, so your host can see that prompt.
- [`private`](/commands/private) is built so the turn is not sent to your host's own model provider.

[What Each Command Protects](/privacy/what-each-command-protects) lays this out command by command.

## The Tier Follows the Model

There is no "private mode" toggle in the plugin. Instead, each model in the catalog has a privacy tier, and you get that tier by choosing that model. Picking a more private model is how you get a more private call. Because the tier is a property of the model, it cannot drift: [Tier Follows the Model](/privacy/tiers) explains why, and what "never silently downgrades" means in practice.

## The Four Tiers, Briefly

Read them as a ladder. Each rung keeps the guarantees below it and adds one:

| Tier | Adds |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Anonymous** | Metadata-only retention by default. Broadest model choice. |
| **Private** | The upstream provider is held to zero data retention. |
| **Confidential** | Your call runs in a sealed environment you can verify with a signed attestation. |
| **Encrypted** | Your data is encrypted to that sealed environment. Not yet available in the plugin. |

Full detail, including what each protects and its trade-offs, is in [The Four Tiers](/privacy/tiers).

## Attested, Not Asserted

The two strongest tiers replace a promise with something you can check. A Confidential call runs inside a sealed environment that produces a **signed attestation**, and you can verify that attestation independently rather than take the claim on trust. That is why this documentation says **attested** and **verifiable**, not "trustless." See [Verify It Yourself](/privacy/tee-attestation).

## Metadata-Only by Default

Even the lightest tier is not a free-for-all. By default Nito keeps operational metadata about a call, not its contents: your prompts, the replies, reasoning, tool payloads, and attachment contents are used to serve the request and are not persisted. [Data Retention and Visibility](/privacy/data-retention-and-visibility) covers exactly what is kept and who can see what.
