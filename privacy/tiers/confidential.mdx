Confidential is the third rung of the [privacy ladder](/privacy/tiers) and the first one backed by hardware you can check. It keeps the guarantees of the levels below it and adds the one thing none of them can: your call runs inside a sealed environment the surrounding system cannot read into, and you can obtain a signed statement that proves it did.

## Runs inside a TEE

Confidential runs your call inside a **Trusted Execution Environment (TEE)**: a sealed environment the surrounding system cannot read into, which produces a signed **attestation** you can verify. New to the concept? Start with the [TEE primer](/privacy/tee-attestation/what-a-tee-is).

That attestation is what separates Confidential from [Private](/privacy/tiers/private). Private holds the provider to zero-data-retention, but you take that on the provider's word (a promise). Confidential gives you an artifact you can independently check (a proof). What Confidential provides is attested and verifiable, and Nito's own verification is a baseline rather than full hardware verification (see below).

## What Confidential Requires

Confidential runs on specific sealed-compute backends only, so not every model can run in a TEE. A model supports it when the catalog marks it as offering attested, sealed execution at the Confidential level. Browse the catalog with the `models` command to see which models carry the Confidential level before you rely on it.

If a model does not carry the Confidential level, choosing it cannot get you sealed execution: the level follows the model, and a request that cannot be served at Confidential fails with a clear message rather than being served at a weaker level.

## How to Select Confidential

There is no privacy flag for Confidential. A model that offers it has its own catalog entry, whose name ends in `:confidential`, and you select that entry the way you select any model. Every model carries its own privacy level, and you get the level of the model you choose. Browse with the `models` command to find models that carry the Confidential level, then set one with `model` (or `--model`). The level follows the model: pick a Confidential-level model and your calls run sealed and attested.

<Note>
Browsing with the `models` command shows each model's privacy level so you can pick a Confidential one. See [Choosing a model](/commands/choosing-a-model) for how to browse and set your model.
</Note>

If the model you choose does not offer Confidential, the call fails rather than quietly downgrading to a weaker level.

## Streaming Works at Confidential

A key reason to choose Confidential over [Encrypted](/privacy/tiers/encrypted): **streaming is supported at Confidential**. You can attest a call and still receive tokens incrementally as they are produced, which a chat interface needs to show output as it arrives. Encrypted's end-to-end encryption, by contrast, is non-streaming by design. So when you need a verifiable execution boundary together with streaming, Confidential is the rung to use.

## Fetching and Verifying the Attestation

The attestation is what makes Confidential verifiable. Nito resolves your model to a sealed, attestation-capable backend, obtains its attestation, and runs a **baseline verification** before handing it back. The baseline checks that the provider evidence is present and parseable, that the proof format and signing material are present, that a fresh value you supply matches the one observed from the provider, and that the binding between them holds. It then returns the result, including the raw evidence, so you can perform **full client-side verification yourself**.

This split matters and is load-bearing: the baseline does **not** perform the full hardware-level checks (the low-level sealed-hardware quotes, accelerator evidence, certificate chains, revocation lists, or trusted-computing-base policies). Those are yours to complete, which is exactly why the raw evidence is handed back to you. Treat the baseline result as a first pass and do the hardware-level verification on your side for maximum assurance. Freshness is caller-driven: supply a fresh random value on each request so the attestation is bound to your call. The full procedure and a reference verifier are covered in [TEE attestation](/privacy/tee-attestation) and its [verify-yourself recipe](/privacy/tee-attestation/verify-yourself-recipe).

Use "attested" and "verifiable" for what Confidential gives you: Nito hands you the raw evidence so you can independently confirm the seal, but you complete that verification yourself.

## Trade-offs

- **Fewer backends.** Sealed execution runs on specific backends only, so Confidential reaches fewer models than Anonymous or Private, which can affect latency and availability.
- **A proof you must use.** The attestation is only worth as much as the verification you do with it. Nito gives you a baseline and the raw evidence; full assurance comes from verifying the evidence yourself.
- **Streaming kept, encryption not yet added.** Confidential is the strongest level that still streams. If you also need your data encrypted to the sealed environment, that is [Encrypted](/privacy/tiers/encrypted), which is non-streaming.

## When to Choose Confidential

Choose Confidential for sensitive calls where you need to verify that a sealed environment ran the work, and where you still want streaming and the normal feature set. It is the sweet spot for high-sensitivity interactive use: attested execution without giving up token-by-token output.
