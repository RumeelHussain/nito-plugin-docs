title: "The Report-Data Binding"
description: "How the report data (signing address plus your nonce) is recomputed and compared when you attest, why anti-replay is caller-driven, and why reports are never persisted."
---

An attestation is only meaningful if it was produced for _your_ request and not replayed from an earlier one. The mechanism that ties a report to a specific request is the **report-data binding**, and the freshness it provides is something you create by sending a fresh nonce. This page explains the binding, how Nito checks it, and exactly where the responsibility for replay protection sits.

## The Binding: report data = signing address plus nonce

Inside a sealed environment, the hardware can stamp a small piece of caller-supplied data into the signed report. That field is the **report data**. Nito uses it to commit the report to two things at once: a signing identity and a nonce.

The binding is, conceptually:

```
report_data = signing_address + nonce
```

The sealed environment binds this value into the report it signs. Because the value includes your nonce, a report produced for one request cannot stand in for another: a different nonce produces different report data, and the signature would not match.

## How the Binding Is Checked

Two separate things happen when the binding is checked, and it is worth keeping them distinct.

1. **The binding is recomputed.** The signing address and the nonce are concatenated in the same signing-address-plus-nonce form, and the result is compared against the report data observed in the evidence. The comparison is **case-insensitive**. If the recomputed value does not match what the report committed to, the binding check fails.
2. **The observed nonce must equal your nonce.** Separately from recomputing the report data, the nonce observed in the evidence is checked against the nonce you supplied. This is a distinct check: one confirms the report committed to the signing address plus the nonce, the other confirms that the nonce in play is the one you actually sent.

Both must hold. Together they establish that the report was produced for a request carrying your nonce and committed to the expected signing address.

## Send a Fresh Nonce Every Time

Here is the part a security reviewer must not miss. **Nothing stores nonces between requests.** There is no memory of nonces already seen, and a nonce is not rejected for having been used before. The freshness guarantee comes entirely from you choosing a fresh, unpredictable nonce every time you attest.

The consequences:

- **Send a fresh random nonce every time** and the binding checks confirm the report is fresh for this request. This is the supported, secure pattern.
- **Omit the nonce** and an **empty-binding report is permitted.** It is allowed, but you get no replay protection from it. An empty binding commits to nothing fresh, so an old report could satisfy it.

So replay protection is not an unconditional property of the attestation. It is a property of the protocol you follow: do not describe a Nito attestation as replay-proof in the abstract. It is replay-proof only when you supply a fresh nonce and verify it was echoed back.

A practical recommendation: generate the nonce from a cryptographically secure random source, at least 32 bytes, encoded as hex, and never reuse one. The [verify-yourself recipe](/privacy/tee-attestation/verify-yourself-recipe) does exactly this as its first step.

## Reports Are Request-Local

Attestation reports are **request-local**. They are produced for the request that asked for them and are **never persisted**. Each attestation stands alone: a fresh nonce, a fresh report, a fresh binding check, with no stored history of past reports. This is consistent with Nito's metadata-only default posture described in the [privacy overview](/privacy/overview).

## How This Fits the Baseline

The binding and the nonce match are gates 4 and 5 of the [seven-gate baseline](/privacy/tee-attestation/seven-gate-baseline), and gate 7 extends the nonce check into the nested payload for accelerator (GPU) envelopes. The address side of the binding is governed by an additional invariant covered in [address-only model binding](/privacy/tee-attestation/address-only-model-binding).
