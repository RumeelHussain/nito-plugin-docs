title: "The Seven-Gate Baseline"
description: "The pass-or-fail checks Nito runs over attestation evidence when you attest a confidential model, enumerated, with the hardware-verification scope limit stated plainly."
---

When you attest a confidential model, Nito fetches the attestation evidence and runs a fixed sequence of checks called the **baseline**. The result is a single `verified` flag on the evidence Nito hands back to you. This page enumerates the seven gates exactly, and then states, as plainly as possible, what the baseline is and is not.

The baseline is a **pass-or-fail contract**. Every gate that applies must pass for the evidence to be marked verified. There is no partial credit and no scoring: a single failed gate means the evidence did not pass the baseline.

## The Seven Gates

#### **Gate 1: Evidence present and parseable.**

The evidence must be present and parseable as a structured object. If there is no evidence, or it does not parse, the baseline fails here.

#### **Gate 2: Self-declared verification.**

If the evidence's own contents declare that it did not verify, the result is marked unverified. Nito respects evidence that says it did not check out on its own terms.

#### **Gate 3: Proof metadata present.**

The proof format, the signing algorithm, and the model public key must all be present in the evidence. Additionally, if you specified a signing algorithm, it must match the algorithm the evidence declares. A requested algorithm that disagrees with the evidence fails this gate.

#### **Gate 4: Nonce match.**

The fresh value (nonce) you supplied must match the nonce observed in the evidence. This is the freshness check at the top level. (If you supply no nonce, see the note on caller-driven anti-replay below and on [the report-data binding page](/privacy/tee-attestation/freshness-binding).)

#### **Gate 5: Report-data binding.**

Nito recomputes the report-data binding and checks that the stamped report data equals the signing address combined with your nonce. It also checks that the observed signing address matches the address you asked about. This is the gate that ties the report to a specific signing identity and a specific nonce. The mechanics are detailed on [the report-data binding page](/privacy/tee-attestation/freshness-binding).

#### **Gate 6: Nested model-attestation binding.**

Beyond the top-level binding, the evidence carries a nested model attestation. Nito checks the binding within it: the model ID, the algorithm, the model signing identity, the model public key, and the nonce. This is where the binding to the model itself (not just the top-level report) is validated. The address-only invariant that governs this gate is covered in [address-only model binding](/privacy/tee-attestation/address-only-model-binding).

#### **Gate 7: Nested payload nonce for accelerator envelopes.**

For evidence that wraps a hardware accelerator (GPU) proof, the nonce inside the nested payload is checked as well. This extends the freshness check into the accelerator-specific portion of the evidence.

## What the Baseline Does NOT Do

This is the most important section on the page, and it is the reason we are careful never to call a baseline-verified result "fully verified."

The baseline does **not**:

- verify the CPU-side hardware quote against a root of trust;
- verify the accelerator (GPU) device-level cryptographic proof;
- validate certificate chains;
- check certificate revocation lists (CRLs);
- evaluate Trusted Computing Base (TCB) policies.

These are exactly the checks that establish full hardware trust, and they are deliberately **out of scope** for the baseline. The baseline confirms that the evidence is structurally sound, internally consistent, freshly bound to your nonce, and committed to a signing identity. It does not confirm that the hardware quote chains back to a genuine, up-to-date hardware root of trust. That is **your responsibility**, performed on the raw evidence Nito returns, through the [verify-yourself path](/privacy/tee-attestation/verify-yourself-recipe).

So the precise meaning of a verified result is: **the evidence passed Nito's baseline contract.** Read it as "baseline-verified," and layer the hardware verification on top yourself.

## Send a Fresh Nonce Every Time

Gate 4 and gate 5 both depend on the nonce. Because nothing stores nonces between requests, the freshness those gates provide is only as strong as the nonce you send. Supply a fresh random nonce every time you attest and the gates enforce that the evidence was produced for this request. Omit the nonce and an empty-binding report is permitted, which means you get no replay protection. This is why every recipe in this section starts by generating a fresh nonce. See [the report-data binding](/privacy/tee-attestation/freshness-binding) for the full treatment.
